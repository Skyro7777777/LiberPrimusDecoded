     4→     4→# The Cipher: A Length-Clocked Progressive Substitution (g per letter, σ per space)
     5→     5→
     6→     6→## Claim
     7→     7→
     8→     8→The unsolved Liber Primus ciphertext (sections 0-9 of `data/page0-58.txt`) is a
     9→     9→**progressive polyalphabetic substitution with a small fixed key**, not a
    10→    10→per-word one-time pad. The alphabet advances by a fixed **order-5 mixed
    11→    11→permutation `g`** on every letter, and by a second fixed **mixed permutation
    12→    12→`σ`** at every word boundary. Per word `w`, at within-word position `j`:
    13→    13→
    14→    14→```
    15→    15→c[j] = base_w( g^(j mod 5)( p[j] ) )
    16→    16→base_{w+1} = base_w ∘ g^((L_w − 1) mod 5) ∘ σ      (L_w = length of word w)
    17→    17→```
    18→    18→
    19→    19→The sequence of per-word bases is generated **deterministically** from the key
    20→    20→`(base_0, g, σ)` and the **public word-length sequence** — the word lengths are
    21→    21→visible in the ciphertext, so they are not secret. Total key: two mixed
    22→    22→29-permutations (one of order 5), **fixed, ~200 bits, not growing with the
    23→    23→text.** This is the load-bearing correction to the earlier `free base →
    24→    24→unbreakable` reading: the base is a keyed generator, so the cipher is breakable
    25→    25→in principle.
    26→    26→
    27→    27→## Status
    28→    28→
    29→    29→**Status**: plausible (comprehensive statistical fit; NOT confirmed by
    30→    30→decryption)
    31→    31→
    32→    32→The model reproduces the base fingerprint (flat unigrams, doublet
    33→    33→suppression, no periodicity) and the d1/d5/d6 profile, and survives the null
    34→    34→battery (below). Two measured anomalies remain **unexplained by the walk**:
    35→    35→the cross-word d4 frame face and the rune-S concentration of the echo (see
    36→    36→"Evidence against / open"). It has **not** been verified by producing
    37→    37→plaintext — the two mixed permutations have not been recovered. Confidence
    38→    38→is high on the *shape* (period-5 mixed substitution + per-word mixed step,
    39→    39→small fixed key), lower on details flagged under "open".

    41→    41→## Mechanism
    42→    42→
    43→    43→Two independent "clocks", forced to be independent (see "single permutation
    44→    44→refuted"):
    45→    45→
    46→    46→- **`g` — the letter step.** Order 5 (`g⁵ = id`), a rich mixed permutation
    47→    47→  (~five 5-cycles + fixed points), tuned **rare-diagonal**: `g(y)` is a rune
    48→    48→  that rarely precedes `y` in plaintext, so the within-word doublet
    49→    49→  (`c[i]=c[i-1] ⟺ p[i-1]=g(p[i])`) is held to 0.0063. `g⁵=id` makes positions
    50→    50→  5 apart share the alphabet → the d5 echo. Non-arithmetic: GF(29)* has order
    51→    51→  28 and 5∤28, so no shift/multiply/affine map has order 5.
    52→    52→  **Nor can the period-5 step be a 5-letter VIGENERE** — the obvious
    53→    53→  simplest reading, and it fails on the doublet rate rather than on
    54→    54→  order (July 2026, `experiments/sigma_algebraic_floor.py`). Under a
    55→    55→  shift schedule the alphabet at position j is `base_w ∘ (add s_j)` with
    56→    56→  s periodic mod 5, so a ciphertext doublet occurs exactly when the
    57→    57→  plaintext adjacent delta equals one phase-specific value. The rarest
    58→    58→  adjacent delta in runeglish is **0.0119** (delta = 18), the best
    59→    59→  5-shift schedule closing to identity (Σs ≡ 0 mod 29, phase-weighted by
    60→    60→  the real adjacency counts) gives **0.0135**, and even granting all
    61→    61→  five phases the single rarest delta the floor is 0.0119 — against an
    62→    62→  observed 0.0063, i.e. **1.9-2.2x too high**. A shift can only dodge
    63→    63→  doublets by exploiting a rare plaintext delta and English has none
    64→    64→  rare enough; a mixed permutation dodges per-rune, routing each image
    65→    65→  to its own rare partner, which is exactly why the cipher needs mixed
    66→    66→  alphabets rather than a keyword. **A MIXED-alphabet (Quagmire)
    67→    67→  Vigenere is a different matter and is NOT excluded**: conjugated
    68→    68→  shifts `K∘(add δ)∘K⁻¹` put the doublet condition on deltas of the
    69→    69→  transformed plaintext `K⁻¹(p)`, which a free `K` reshapes — jointly
    70→    70→  optimising `K` and five offsets reaches 0.0017. The 12-keyword sample
    71→    71→  that once closed the keyword version was superseded by the
    72→    72→  full-dictionary exhaustion: **12,064 keyword alphabets clear the
    73→    73→  within-word diagonal** (best 0.0010), so the enumerable-key version
    74→    74→  is LIVE — see `mixed-alphabet-vigenere.md`. What stands of the tuning
    75→    75→  rule is narrower: the two exhaustive floor computations (plain
    76→    76→  shifts, the arithmetic families) show the relation cannot be
    77→    77→  arithmetic; a merely-mixed alphabet is not excluded — ~1.5% of
    78→    78→  random mixed alphabets clear the target — it is merely atypical.
    79→    79→  **A tuned-diagonal-free reformulation is refuted** — advance-4/hold-1
    80→    80→  (`g4⁴=id`), where the hold exposes 1/5 of plaintext doublets, predicted
    81→    81→  the doublet rate parameter-free but is DISPROVED by the doublet
    82→    82→  position-profile test (July 2026): the doublets are positionally flat,
    83→    83→  not plaintext-double-shaped. The diagonal is genuinely tuned, and the
    84→    84→  profile adds a key constraint: the diagonal bigram class {(g(y), y)}
    85→    85→  must itself be positionally near-baseline in plaintext. See
    86→    86→  `stay-slot-hold.md`, `experiments/doublet_position_profile.py`.
    87→    87→- **`σ` — the space step.** A second mixed permutation **outside ⟨g⟩** (not a
    88→    88→  power of `g`), tuned rare-diagonal so the seam doublet is suppressed to
    89→    89→  0.0079. Being outside ⟨g⟩ is what breaks the walk out of the 5-alphabet
    90→    90→  cyclic cage and gives flat unigrams + aperiodicity. This is now
    91→    91→  quantitative (`sigma-power-step.md`): σ = g^k is disproved for every k
    92→    92→  (identity-census collapse, ~2,500 predicted identical cipher words vs 17
    93→    93→  observed; DJU-BEI arithmetic forbids k ≠ 1 and periodicity kills k = 1),
    94→    94→  near-powers fail too, ⟨g,σ⟩ must reach N ≳ 600 bases, and the seam
    95→    95→  algebra isolates σ exactly: a cross-word doublet ⟺ p_last = σ(p_first),
    96→    96→  the `g` and length factors cancelling.
    97→    97→
    98→    98→**No closed form for the base (probably).** Four words in, the base is
    99→    99→the raw alternating word `base_0 g^{a₀} σ g^{a₁} σ g^{a₂} σ g^{a₃} σ`,
   100→   100→which does not simplify. It would if σ normalised ⟨g⟩ (σgσ⁻¹ = g^r),
   101→   101→giving `base_w = base_0 ∘ g^(Σ aᵢ rⁱ mod 5) ∘ σ^w` — but that caps the
   102→   102→state at 5·ord(σ), and exhaustive enumeration of the normalizer bounds
   103→   103→that at 300 bases for a twisted σ (comfortably under the ~600 the
   104→   104→identity census wants) and 500 for a commuting one (marginal: the
   105→   105→census figure is itself a 2σ bound, and at 3σ it falls to ~490). So the
   106→   106→twisted cases are excluded and the commuting case is disfavoured rather
   107→   107→than dead. See `sigma-power-step.md`.
   108→   108→
   109→   109→The `g^((L−1) mod 5)` factor in the transition completes the period-5 cycle at
   110→   110→the boundary, so the letter-clock effectively runs continuously across the
   111→   111→space; that is why doublet suppression is boundary-blind. Note the
   112→   112→period-5-vs-continuous framing is a **reparametrization** (fold `g^(word_start
   113→   113→mod 5)` into the base), so "does the phase reset per word?" is not observable.

   312→   312→  partial-echo question is untouched and still undecidable (corrected
   313→   313→  φ5 = 0.85 ± 0.26); d5 remains predicted 0.0565 against observed 0.0492,
   314→   314→  which is the register/leak question, not a d6 question.
   315→   315→- **Order-5-g vs stay-slot: SEPARATED (July 2026).** Direct simulation
   316→   316→  on real runeglish words (`mechanism_discriminator.py`) could not separate
   317→   317→  them — both reproduce the d5 echo and d1≠d6, and the d1..d6 fit winner
   318→   318→  flips with the g-tuning seed. The doublet position-profile test now
   319→   319→  does: stay-slot forces the doublets to be plaintext double letters
   320→   320→  (start-forbidden, end-heavy), and the observed doublets are positionally
   321→   321→  flat — stay-slot disproved, tuned order-5-g survives with the new
   322→   322→  diagonal-class constraint above. What's real beyond period-5 is the
   323→   323→  **damped rising shoulder** — LP's d2→d5 tracks a half-damped copy of the
   324→   324→  plaintext's own within-word profile with extra phase-1 suppression, i.e. a
   325→   325→  partial plaintext leak under the period-5 envelope. See
   326→   326→  `d5-partial-alphabet-leak.md`.
   327→   327→- **Continuous-vs-per-section walk: weak.** Page-seam doublets 0/47 favour
   328→   328→  "continuous" ~3.4:1; DJU-BEI's differing section-offsets argue against
   329→   329→  per-section reset. But only 9 section boundaries — not provable.
   330→   330→- **The d4 frame face is not produced by the walk.** Of the lag-5 paired
   331→   331→  matches, the d1 face (`XY···XY`) is plaintext morphology passing through
   332→   332→  the `g⁵=id` echo — accounted for. The d4 face ((1st,5th)-of-5 frame,
   333→   333→  largely cross-word) is BELOW chance in real runeglish plaintext, so the
   334→   334→  echo cannot pass it through. The July 2026 anatomy
   335→   335→  (`experiments/d4_frame_anatomy.py`) found no walk-compatible structure
   336→   336→  in it either (most legs cross one boundary, where alphabet coincidence
   337→   337→  is impossible given σ ∉ ⟨g⟩; no σ-relation geometry cell enriched), so
   338→   338→  it stands as a count excess compatible with scan noise rather than
   339→   339→  as evidence against the model. See `lag5-digraph-structure.md`.
   340→   340→- **The rune-S echo — resolved, no tension (July 2026).** 11 of the 102
   341→   341→  within-word d5 pairs echo the single rune S (p=2.4e-6 against a
   342→   342→  frequency-weighted null). That null is wrong for this model: under the
   343→   343→  walk the echoed rune is base-scrambled per word, identities are
   344→   344→  near-uniform and cluster by word, and simulation gives
   345→   345→  P(max ≥ 11) = 0.08-0.15 (`experiments/rune_s_walk_test.py`) — a
   346→   346→  1-in-12 event, not a strike. The concentration still refutes
   347→   347→  identity-preserving mechanisms; the fixed-channel variant is excluded
   348→   348→  by unigram arithmetic. See `rune-s-lag5-echo.md`.
   349→   349→- **Keyword-grid keys are excluded.** `experiments/enumerate_keys.py` drove
   350→   350→  keyword-grid `g` × keyword `σ` through the DJU-BEI/diagonal cascade
   351→   351→  (`experiments/walk_verifier.py`): 0/480 parity-valid pairs give a state
   352→   352→  return, and the best keyword-grid `g` diagonal in that scan was 0.023 vs
   353→   353→  the required 0.0063 — **the diagonal exclusion is RETRACTED** (July 2026,
   354→   354→  `experiments/g_construction_survey.py`): the scan fixed the four fixed
   355→   355→  runes as the last four of the keyword order and rotated every column by
   356→   356→  one; freeing those two parameters lets every ordering and fill tested
   357→   357→  reach the required band (~2x10⁵ in-band candidates from a single
   358→   358→  keyword), so the state-return result must be re-run over the corrected
   359→   359→  family. The retraction is of the letter, not the spirit: a grid-derived
   360→   360→  `g` has the diagonal distribution of a random order-5 permutation
   361→   361→  (mean 0.0345 vs 0.0346; 0.17% vs 0.07% at or below 0.0063), so the
   362→   362→  construction supplies no low diagonal — the family merely contains
   363→   363→  in-band members at a ~1-in-600 rate because it is large. **Open problem, now inverted**: the structured family is not
   364→   364→  too poor to supply `g`, it is too RICH to enumerate — ~10⁹-10¹⁰ in-band
   365→   365→  `g` candidates across plausible keywords and fills, before `σ`, which
   366→   366→  has no construction proposal and cannot be hill-climbed. The diagonal
   367→   367→  band is too weak a filter on its own; progress needs the constraints
   368→   368→  applied JOINTLY (g² near background, positional profile, σ's
   369→   369→  cross-word diagonal, the DJU-BEI state return — the parity and
   370→   370→  abelianization shortcuts are vacuous, `mixed-alphabet-vigenere.md`)
   371→   371→  or a principle that pins the
   372→   372→  fixed-rune choice and the column rotations. See `g-from-5x5-grid.md`.
   373→   373→- **Is `g` provably FIXED? No — but the alternatives cost key material
   374→   374→  without buying anything (July 2026).** What the ciphertext actually
   375→   375→  requires is local: the composition of the five steps spanning positions
   376→   376→  j and j+5 *inside one word* must be the identity, and the step relation
   377→   377→  must be rare-diagonal. Nothing tests whether the same `g` is in force
   378→   378→  from word to word — the echo is within-word, the seam algebra cancels
   379→   379→  `g` entirely (a seam doublet is `p_last = σ(p_first)`, no `g` in it),
   380→   380→  and the base schedule is unobservable. A per-word family `g_w` would
   381→   381→  be invisible to every battery run here, PROVIDED each `g_w` has order 5
   382→   382→  and a rare diagonal. What excludes it is design economy rather than
   383→   383→  statistics: selecting from that family per word is a schedule, i.e. key
   384→   384→  material growing with the text, which slides the cipher toward an OTP
   385→   385→  and destroys the small-fixed-key property that makes it readable at all.
   386→   386→  The natural middle case — a CYCLIC SCHEDULE of five distinct steps
   387→   387→  `a₁…a₅` with `a₁a₂a₃a₄a₅ = id`, applied in rotation (fixed `g` is the
   388→   388→  special case `a_i = g`) — preserves the echo exactly at every phase
   389→   389→  (cyclic rotations of an identity product are conjugates of the
   390→   390→  identity), but simulation shows it neither helps nor is free: the d1-d8
   391→   391→  profile matches the fixed-`g` model (d4 0.034 vs LP 0.041, d6 0.031 vs
   392→   392→  LP 0.025 — the same two failures), and the product constraint leaves
   393→   393→  only FOUR steps free, forcing the fifth as the inverse of the others'
   394→   394→  product with an untuned diagonal (~0.013 in the run) that drags the
   395→   395→  doublet rate up. Fixed `g` tunes one diagonal once and uses it five
   396→   396→  times. So `g` is fixed by parsimony and tuning economy, not by proof.
   397→   397→- **`σ`: one or a small keyed set?** Seam suppression proves the space step is
   398→   398→  a rare-diagonal permutation (not a random re-key) but does not prove there is
   399→   399→  only *one* `σ`. A small keyed family fits equally; the fully-deterministic
   400→   400→  single-`σ` version is the simplest, not the confirmed, form.
   401→   401→- **Not confirmed by decryption.** All of the above is statistical shape.
   402→   402→
   403→   403→## Predictions
   404→   404→
   405→   405→- The key `(base_0, g, σ)` is small and fixed; decryption is a deterministic
   406→   406→  function of it and the observable word lengths. A **contiguous crib** of a
   407→   407→  few words would pin `g` and `σ` and then propagate to the whole corpus —
   408→   408→  unlike a free-base model, where a crib dies at its own word.
   409→   409→- Any candidate key must satisfy the DJU-BEI **state return** `base_1477 =
   410→   410→  base_2926`. This is the full 1,449-step composition and is expensive; the
   411→   411→  abelianization relation below is NOT a cheap stand-in for it.
   412→   412→- A single guessed word (e.g. DJU-BEI = a [3,3] phrase such as "OUR OWN") is
   413→   413→  **not** verifiable — 6 runes under two unknown bases + g is underdetermined.
   414→   414→- **The 2-rune words are the sharpest available key discriminator
   415→   415→  (July 2026).** In runeglish `THE` is exactly two runes (`ᚦᛖ`), and
   416→   416→  2-rune words are a dense, highly concentrated class: in the register
   417→   417→  corpus they are 22.8% of all tokens and their content is dominated by
   418→   418→  a handful of function words — THE 16%, TO 15%, OF 13%, IN 7%, IT 5%,
   419→   419→  HE 5%, BE 4%, AS 4% (top eight = 69%). The LP has **465** 2-rune
   420→   420→  words, so an English-like plaintext puts roughly **75-108 instances of
   421→   421→  THE** among them (75 if the 2-rune class has register composition, 108
   422→   422→  if THE keeps its 3.7% token rate). A 2-rune word needs only `base_w`
   423→   423→  and `g` to decrypt (`p₀ = base_w⁻¹(c₀)`, `p₁ = g⁻¹(base_w⁻¹(c₁))`), so
   424→   424→  every candidate key can be scored by decrypting all 465 and counting
   425→   425→  `ᚦᛖ`: **~75-108 hits for a correct key against 465/812 ≈ 0.6 by
   426→   426→  chance** — a discriminator of order 100x, far sharper than quadgram
   427→   427→  fitness. For hill-climbing, the count alone is too peaked to give a
   428→   428→  gradient; the usable objective is the log-likelihood of the decrypted
   429→   429→  2-rune words against the whole register distribution above, which
   430→   430→  awards partial credit for TO, OF, IN … while the key is still wrong.
   431→   431→  Caveat and cross-check: the LP's 2-rune share is 15.9%, well below the
   432→   432→  register's 22.8% — the documented short-word deficit
   433→   433→  (`word-length-keystream-and-boundaries.md`) — so either the plaintext
   434→   434→  register is unusually short-word-poor, or the boundary question bears
   435→   435→  on it; the expected-hit range above already spans that uncertainty.
   436→   436→  **Validated on planted keys** (`experiments/two_rune_gradient.py`):
   437→   437→  the objective recovers `base_0` exactly and instantly once `g` and `σ`
   438→   438→  are known, but the landscape over `(g, σ)` fails from both ends —
   439→   439→  short prefixes keep a wide basin but cannot pin a 29-permutation,
   440→   440→  long ones discriminate but the basin collapses. So this is a verifier,
   441→   441→  not a search gradient, and `base_0` should be treated as free rather
   442→   442→  than as part of the key search. See `no-known-plaintext-foothold.md`.

   467→   467→## Verdict
   468→   468→
   469→   469→The mechanism is characterized: a **length-clocked progressive substitution** —
   470→   470→step `g` (order-5 mixed) per letter, step `σ` (mixed, outside ⟨g⟩) per space,
   471→   471→base = the running product clocked by the public word lengths. Both steps are
   472→   472→non-arithmetic mixed permutations, which would be a first for Cicada and
   473→   473→explains why value-based attacks fail. The key is **small and fixed**, so the
   474→   474→cipher is breakable in principle — but the barrier is recovering two mixed
   475→   475→29-permutations, against which every statistical and algebraic shortcut
   476→   476→tried so far is null. The model does not account for the d4 frame face or
   477→   477→the rune-S echo; either could falsify or refine it. No hillclimb over
   478→   478→`(g, σ)` can work — the two-rune objective proved the landscape is a
   479→   479→delta function (`no-known-plaintext-foothold.md`) — so the realistic
   480→   480→paths to plaintext are enumeration of structured candidates verified
   481→   481→per key (the keyword Quagmire family of `mixed-alphabet-vigenere.md`
   482→   482→is the one enumerable set) or a contiguous crib; a short guessed
   483→   483→phrase alone cannot verify. Everything here is statistical
   484→   484→shape, not a confirmed decryption.
