    85→### 3.2 The 3 features I had NOT tested
    86→
    87→File: `/home/z/my-project/cicada3301-research/solvers/lp-decrypter/enc/DecryptModel.py` (~825 lines)
    88→
    89→```python
    90→# Pseudocode from main_loop_rune():
    91→for ct in ct_words:                              # Loop over ciphertext windows
    92→    for key in keys_list:                        # Loop over user-supplied key candidates
    93→        for ct_start_index in range(0, max_start):    # [N3] KEY DRAGGING — rotate CT
    94→            for interrupter in interrupters_p:        # [N3a] Try each of 29 runes as interrupter

   101→
   102→- **[N3a] Interrupter enumeration** — tries every rune (0-28) as a "literal pause-the-key" marker, AND for each interrupter tries every combination of positions where it appears in the CT. For a 200-rune window with 5 occurrences of rune R, this gives 2^5 = 32 position sets per interrupter per CT window.
   103→- **[N3b] CT-side gematria rotations** — applies {forward, reverse} × {0..28} shifts = 58 transformations to the CT before decryption.
   104→- **[N3c] Key-side gematria rotations** — same 58 transformations to the key.
   105→- **Combined search space**: 8 functions × 29 interrupters × 32 position sets × 58 CT rotations × 58 key rotations × N keys × N drag positions ≈ 2.5 × 10^9 per page even before 4-gram scoring. This is the search space I have NOT exhaustively covered.

   170→### 4.4 The lag-5 paired coincidence phenomenon (the most important new finding)
   171→
   172→**Documented in** `aldegonde/docs/lag5-phenomenon.md` (research note, June 2026).
   173→
   174→**Definition:** M[i] = 1 iff C[i] = C[i+5]. A "d1 event" is a pair M[i]=M[i+1]=1 (digraph repeats 5 later). A "d4 event" is M[i]=M[i+4]=1 (consecutive 5-grams agree in their 1st and 5th positions).
   175→
   176→**Observation:** Across the 12,956 unsolved runes:
   177→
   178→| statistic | observed | uniform expectation |
   179→|-----------|----------|---------------------|
   180→| Σ M (mono lag-5 matches) | 479 | 446.6 (+1.5σ, unremarkable) |
   181→| pairs at separation d=1 | **29** | 15.4 |
   182→| pairs at separation d=2 | 15 | 15.4 |
   183→| pairs at separation d=3 | 14 | 15.4 |
   184→| pairs at separation d=4 | **28** | 15.4 |
   185→| pairs at separation d=5 | 19 | 15.4 |
   186→| separations 6–25 | flat | 15.4 |
   187→
   188→**Significance:** Family-blind Monte Carlo p ≈ 0.033 (1-in-30 false-fluke rate under the fair test). Honest significance = lead, not proof.
   189→
   190→**Why a decade of analysis missed it:** It is a **fourth-order correlation** (relates two coincidence events at adjacent offsets = 4 text positions jointly). Standard tests (IoC, kappa, Friedman, bigram tables, mutual information, autocorrelation, spectral tests) are all 1st/2nd-order and are mathematically incapable of seeing it. LP is verified flat at orders 1-3; the ONLY non-trivial structure is this order-4 anomaly plus the well-known order-2 doublet suppression.
   191→
   192→**Precedent:** Zodiac-340 was cracked starting from the same statistic family.
   193→
   194→**Three interpretations** (information-theoretic constraint — copied runes carry no plaintext info):
   195→1. **Nulls** — copied runes encode nothing, decryptor skips them
   196→2. **Coincidence** — keystream repeated AND plaintext repeated (rate-disfavored)
   197→3. **Back-references** — events fire only where plaintext repeats at lag 5 ("repeat the previous digraph/frame") — feasible but unverified
   198→
   199→**Wave-7 action item:** Test interpretation (3) by using the ~114 crib equations (P[i] = P[i−5] at marked positions) as input to a key-search attack. This is an entirely new cryptanalytic attack surface I have not touched.

   201→### 4.5 The doublet-suppression mathematical floor (also new)
   202→
   203→**Source:** `aldegonde/docs/lp_structure_findings.md` §3
   204→
   205→For any additive cipher `c = p + k (mod 29)` whose keystream is generated independently of the plaintext (periodic Vigenère, Beaufort, running key, Gronsfeld, any PRNG/mathematical stream — *regardless of key length*):
   206→
   207→```
   208→P(c[i+1]=c[i]) = Σ_d P(Δp = −d) · P(Δk = d)  ≥  min_d P(Δp = d) ≈ 1.7 %
   209→```
   210→
   211→The runeglish lag-1 difference distribution has smallest bin ≈ 1.7%. The LP's observed 0.66% is **half the theoretical floor**. Hence:
   212→
   213→**No plaintext-independent additive keystream can produce this ciphertext.**
   214→
   215→This single theorem mathematically refutes the entire class of additive cipher with independent keystream — i.e., refutes the Wave-4 hash-as-keystream, Wave-5 PRNG-keystream, and ALL running-key variants BEFORE any key search. My Wave-1..5 campaigns were doomed by construction.

   293→`runApplyKey` implements Vigenère-style Add/Subtract with 3 keystream schemes:
   294→

   297→if (scheme !== "Standard" && !(nullPreserving && (o === 0 || o === k))) {
   298→    switch (scheme) {

   300→            key[i % key.length] = x;  // x = output (just-decrypted plaintext)
